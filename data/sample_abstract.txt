BACKGROUND: Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists reduce cardiovascular events in type 2 diabetes, but their renal efficacy in advanced chronic kidney disease requires further evaluation.
METHODS: In this randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, 3533 patients with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease (eGFR 25 to 75 ml/min/1.73 m2 and UACR >300 mg/g) were assigned to subcutaneous semaglutide (1.0 mg weekly) or placebo. The primary endpoint was major kidney disease events (onset of kidney failure, >=50% reduction in eGFR, or death from kidney/cardiovascular causes).
RESULTS: During a median follow-up of 3.4 years, the primary endpoint occurred in 331 of 1767 participants (18.7%) in the semaglutide group and 410 of 1766 participants (23.2%) in the placebo group (hazard ratio, 0.76; 95% CI, 0.66 to 0.88; P = 0.0001). Risk of major cardiovascular events was 18% lower in the semaglutide group (HR 0.82; 95% CI 0.68 to 0.98; P = 0.029). Serious adverse events were reported in 49.6% vs 53.8% of patients.
CONCLUSIONS: Once-weekly semaglutide significantly reduced the risk of clinically important kidney outcomes and cardiovascular mortality in patients with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease.
